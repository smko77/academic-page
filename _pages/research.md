I am a PhD student in mathematics at Columbia University, advised by [Andrew Blumberg](https://ajblumberg.github.io/). My research lies at the intersection of chromatic homotopy theory and algebraic K-theory.

## Path here

As an undergraduate at POSTECH, my interests were in algebraic and arithmetic geometry, and this continued into my first year of graduate school. I became interested in homotopy theory after reading the work of Bhatt&ndash;Morrow&ndash;Scholze, which introduces a motivic filtration on topological Hochschild homology (THH) and its variants whose graded pieces are related to prismatic and syntomic cohomology. From there, my interests gradually expanded into algebraic K-theory and chromatic homotopy theory, which is where I currently work.

## Current interests

I am thinking about Waldhausen's chromatic convergence for K-theory via the Efimov K-theory of dualizable categories, and about the chromatic behavior of the algebraic K-theory of various ring spectra, using the even filtration of Hahn&ndash;Raksit&ndash;Wilson as a tool. I am also curious about how chromatic phenomena, trace methods, and motivic filtrations behave for equivariant and hermitian K-theory.

## How I like to learn

Much of how I learn is by organizing seminars around topics I want to understand properly. At Columbia I have co-organized graduate seminars on stable homotopy theory, prismatic cohomology, chromatic homotopy theory, and — most recently — manifolds and homotopy theory, with a current focus on homological stability and the cellular E<sub>k</sub>-algebras of Galatius&ndash;Kupers&ndash;Randal-Williams. See the [seminars page](/seminars/) for the full list and talk abstracts.
